# Landscaping Visualization Prompts

Sample prompts for virtual staging projects focused on landscaping, gardening, and outdoor spaces. Use these as the **style direction** when creating a staging project — the AI will adapt them to your specific photos.

---

## Foundation Plantings & Borders

**Evergreen Foundation**
> Add a row of boxwood hedges along the house foundation, with dwarf Alberta spruce at each corner. Place river rock mulch beneath the plantings and a few solar path lights along the edge.

**Cottage Garden Border**
> Plant a layered flower border along the fence: tall hollyhocks and delphiniums in back, mid-height lavender and Russian sage in the middle, low creeping thyme and alyssum spilling onto the walkway.

**Drought-Tolerant Xeriscape**
> Replace the grass strip with decomposed granite, clusters of agave and yucca, purple sage, red kangaroo paw, and a single large desert willow tree. Add a few terracotta pots with barrel cactus.

---

## Trees & Canopy

**Japanese Maple Focal Point**
> Place a mature Japanese maple (Acer palmatum 'Bloodgood') with deep red foliage in the front yard corner. Surround its base with mondo grass and a ring of natural stepping stones.

**Shade Tree Canopy**
> Add two large live oaks to frame the backyard, with a hammock strung between them. Plant hostas, ferns, and astilbe in the dappled shade beneath.

**Fruit Tree Orchard Row**
> Plant a row of three semi-dwarf fruit trees along the back fence: a Meyer lemon, a Fuji apple, and a Santa Rosa plum, each with a circle of wood chip mulch at the base.

**Privacy Screen**
> Add a dense row of Leyland cypress trees along the property line, spaced 6 feet apart, forming a tall green privacy wall. Place ornamental grasses at their feet.

---

## Garden Beds & Flower Displays

**Pollinator Garden**
> Create a naturalistic garden bed with purple coneflower, black-eyed Susan, bee balm, milkweed, and butterfly bush. Include a shallow birdbath and a few flat stones for butterflies to bask on.

**Rose Garden**
> Plant a formal rose garden with climbing 'New Dawn' roses on a white arbor, floribunda 'Iceberg' roses in symmetric beds, and a gravel path with boxwood edging running through the center.

**Tropical Paradise**
> Add large bird of paradise plants, red hibiscus, plumeria, giant elephant ears, and a tall traveler's palm. Place river rocks and a small recirculating bamboo water feature among the plantings.

**Succulent Rock Garden**
> Arrange a sloped rock garden with echeveria rosettes, trailing string of pearls, blue chalk sticks, and aeonium. Intersperse with smooth boulders and pea gravel ground cover.

---

## Hardscaping + Planting Combos

**Flagstone Patio with Herb Garden**
> Add a natural flagstone patio with creeping thyme growing between the joints. Place raised cedar planters along one edge filled with rosemary, basil, thyme, oregano, and chives.

**Stone Retaining Wall with Cascading Plants**
> Build a dry-stack stone retaining wall along the slope. Plant cascading rosemary, trailing lantana, and purple aubrieta spilling over the wall face. Add lavender and salvia behind the wall.

**Fire Pit Garden**
> Place a circular stone fire pit surrounded by pea gravel. Around the perimeter, plant ornamental grasses (Karl Foerster and purple fountain grass), with Adirondack chairs between the grass clusters.

---

## Lawn & Ground Cover Alternatives

**Clover Lawn**
> Replace the patchy grass with a lush micro-clover lawn, bordered by a natural edge of creeping phlox and wild violets. Add a winding stepping stone path of round slate pavers.

**Moss Garden**
> Transform the shaded area into a moss garden with sheet moss, fern moss, and cushion moss between natural boulders. Add a single stone lantern and a small tsukubai water basin.

**Meadow Wildflowers**
> Convert the open yard into a wildflower meadow with California poppies, lupine, cosmos, and native grasses. Mow a single curved path through the center.

---

## Vertical & Container Gardens

**Living Wall**
> Install a vertical garden panel on the bare fence wall with trailing pothos, ferns, succulents, and flowering bromeliads. Add uplighting at the base.

**Container Patio Garden**
> Arrange large ceramic pots on the patio with dwarf Japanese maple, ornamental grasses, trailing sweet potato vine, and seasonal flowers. Vary pot heights and colors for visual interest.

**Hanging Basket Display**
> Add hanging baskets along the porch overhang with trailing fuchsia, lobelia, ivy geraniums, and Boston ferns. Include a few shepherd's hooks with baskets in the garden bed.

---

## Water Features & Structures

**Pond Garden**
> Add a small naturalistic pond with water lilies, iris, and papyrus along the edges. Place flat fieldstones around the rim and a few koi-colored fish visible in the clear water.

**Dry Creek Bed**
> Create a dry creek bed winding through the yard using smooth river rocks of varying sizes, flanked by blue fescue grass, dwarf nandina, and Japanese forest grass.

**Pergola with Climbing Vines**
> Place a cedar pergola over the patio with wisteria and star jasmine climbing the posts and trailing across the top beams. Add string lights woven through the vines.

---

## Seasonal Variations

**Spring Bloom**
> Add masses of tulips, daffodils, and hyacinths in the front beds. Place flowering cherry trees with pink blossoms flanking the walkway and a ground cover of creeping phlox in pink and white.

**Fall Color**
> Show the yard in peak autumn: burning bush shrubs in crimson, ornamental grasses in golden plumes, chrysanthemums in orange and burgundy pots on the porch steps, and a sugar maple dropping amber leaves.

**Winter Interest**
> Add plants with winter structure: red-twig dogwood, winterberry holly with bright red berries, ornamental kale in the planters, and a dusting of frost on evergreen topiaries.

---

## Tips for Best Results

- **Be specific about plant species** — "Japanese maple (Acer palmatum 'Bloodgood')" gives better results than "red tree"
- **Reference placement** — "along the fence," "in the corner," "flanking the walkway" helps the AI position plants naturally
- **Include scale cues** — "dwarf," "mature," "6-foot tall" helps with realistic proportions
- **Layer heights** — mention tall, medium, and low plants for natural-looking compositions
- **Add non-plant elements** — mulch, rocks, lighting, and furniture make scenes feel complete
