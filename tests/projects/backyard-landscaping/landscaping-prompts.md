# Landscaping Visualization Prompts

Sample prompts for virtual staging projects focused on landscaping, gardening, and outdoor spaces. Use these with the **Projects** feature — upload your photos as a baseline collection, write a prompt, and generate a portfolio of visual possibilities.

---

## How to Use These Prompts

### Starting a New Project

1. Go to **Projects → New Project**
2. Name your project (e.g., "Backyard Fence Line — Spring 2026")
3. Upload all your baseline photos — shoot every angle of the area you want to visualize (fence lines, borders, yard sections, patio edges). The more photos you upload, the more complete your portfolio.
4. Pick a prompt from below (or combine several) as your **style direction**
5. Hit **Generate** — the AI will adapt your prompt to each photo, placing plants and objects where they naturally fit in that specific scene

### Building a Portfolio of Possibilities

One project gives you one style direction across all your photos. To explore different looks:

- **Create multiple projects** using the same baseline photos but different prompts — e.g., one project for "Tropical fence line," another for "Native pollinator border," another for "Evergreen privacy screen"
- **Add more photos later** — after your first round, go back and upload additional angles or new areas of your yard. Hit "Regenerate All" to get fresh variations that include the new images.
- **Iterate on prompts** — if the first round is close but not quite right, create a new project with a refined prompt. For example, go from "add plants along the fence" to "add layered plantings along the fence: tall ornamental grasses in back, mid-height hydrangeas, low spreading juniper in front"
- **Mix and match** — after generating several projects, save your favorite individual variations to the gallery to build a curated lookbook of the best options across all your experiments

### Prompt Writing Tips

- **Reference your upload** — start with "I'm uploading photos of my backyard fence line" or "These are baseline images of my front yard border" so the AI understands the context
- **Be specific about plant species** — "Japanese maple (Acer palmatum 'Bloodgood')" beats "red tree"
- **Describe placement** — "along the fence," "in the corners," "flanking the gate" helps position plants naturally
- **Layer heights** — always mention tall (back), medium (middle), and low (front) for realistic depth
- **Include non-plant elements** — mulch type, edging, lighting, and hardscaping complete the scene
- **State what to keep** — "keep the existing patio and shed exactly as they are" ensures the AI only adds, never removes

## Fence Line & Border Plantings

These prompts are designed for when you upload photos of your yard edges, fence lines, and property borders.

**Layered Privacy Fence Border**
> I'm uploading baseline photos of my backyard fence line. Add layered plantings along the entire fence: tall Leyland cypress (8-10 ft) and arborvitae in the back row for privacy, mid-height hydrangea (Hydrangea paniculata 'Limelight') and knockout roses in the middle, and low-spreading creeping juniper and liriope as ground cover in front. Fill gaps with mulch and add a few solar bollard lights along the border.

**Native Pollinator Fence Border**
> These are photos of my fence edges. Visualize a naturalistic pollinator garden running along the fence: tall Joe-Pye weed and ironweed against the fence, mid-height purple coneflower (Echinacea purpurea), black-eyed Susan (Rudbineckia), and bee balm in the middle layer, and low native sedges and wild strawberry as ground cover. Keep the plantings loose and meadow-like, not formal.

**Tropical Fence Line**
> I'm uploading images of my backyard fences. Add a tropical border: tall banana plants and bird of paradise against the fence, mid-height plumeria, croton, and hibiscus in front of those, and low bromeliads and mondo grass along the front edge. Add river rock mulch between plantings and a few landscape boulders for structure.

**Ornamental Grass Fence Screen**
> Uploading photos of my property line fence. Layer ornamental grasses along it: tall Miscanthus 'Morning Light' (6-8 ft) in the back, mid-height Karl Foerster feather reed grass in the middle, and low blue fescue mounds in front. Add a 3-inch layer of brown bark mulch throughout.

**Evergreen + Flowering Mix**
> These are baseline images of my side-yard fence. Plant a mix along the fence line: tall emerald green arborvitae every 4 feet for year-round structure, fill between them with mid-height Knock Out roses (red) and dwarf butterfly bush, and edge the front with low boxwood globes and purple catmint (Nepeta). Add landscape fabric and cedar mulch.

**Four-Season Fence Border**
> I'm uploading all angles of my back fence. Design a border that looks good year-round: tall deciduous holly (Ilex verticillata) with winter berries and red-twig dogwood for winter interest, spring-blooming azaleas and forsythia for mid-height, summer black-eyed Susans and daylilies, and fall-coloring Itea (sweetspire) along the front. Evergreen boxwood anchors each end.

## Foundation Plantings & Borders

**Evergreen Foundation**
> Add a row of boxwood hedges along the house foundation, with dwarf Alberta spruce at each corner. Place river rock mulch beneath the plantings and a few solar path lights along the edge.

**Cottage Garden Border**
> Plant a layered flower border along the fence: tall hollyhocks and delphiniums in back, mid-height lavender and Russian sage in the middle, low creeping thyme and alyssum spilling onto the walkway.

**Drought-Tolerant Xeriscape**
> Replace the grass strip with decomposed granite, clusters of agave and yucca, purple sage, red kangaroo paw, and a single large desert willow tree. Add a few terracotta pots with barrel cactus.

## Trees & Canopy

**Japanese Maple Focal Point**
> Place a mature Japanese maple (Acer palmatum 'Bloodgood') with deep red foliage in the front yard corner. Surround its base with mondo grass and a ring of natural stepping stones.

**Shade Tree Canopy**
> Add two large live oaks to frame the backyard, with a hammock strung between them. Plant hostas, ferns, and astilbe in the dappled shade beneath.

**Fruit Tree Orchard Row**
> Plant a row of three semi-dwarf fruit trees along the back fence: a Meyer lemon, a Fuji apple, and a Santa Rosa plum, each with a circle of wood chip mulch at the base.

**Privacy Screen**
> Add a dense row of Leyland cypress trees along the property line, spaced 6 feet apart, forming a tall green privacy wall. Place ornamental grasses at their feet.

---

## Garden Beds & Flower Displays

**Pollinator Garden**
> Create a naturalistic garden bed with purple coneflower, black-eyed Susan, bee balm, milkweed, and butterfly bush. Include a shallow birdbath and a few flat stones for butterflies to bask on.

**Rose Garden**
> Plant a formal rose garden with climbing 'New Dawn' roses on a white arbor, floribunda 'Iceberg' roses in symmetric beds, and a gravel path with boxwood edging running through the center.

**Tropical Paradise**
> Add large bird of paradise plants, red hibiscus, plumeria, giant elephant ears, and a tall traveler's palm. Place river rocks and a small recirculating bamboo water feature among the plantings.

**Succulent Rock Garden**
> Arrange a sloped rock garden with echeveria rosettes, trailing string of pearls, blue chalk sticks, and aeonium. Intersperse with smooth boulders and pea gravel ground cover.

## Hardscaping + Planting Combos

**Flagstone Patio with Herb Garden**
> Add a natural flagstone patio with creeping thyme growing between the joints. Place raised cedar planters along one edge filled with rosemary, basil, thyme, oregano, and chives.

**Stone Retaining Wall with Cascading Plants**
> Build a dry-stack stone retaining wall along the slope. Plant cascading rosemary, trailing lantana, and purple aubrieta spilling over the wall face. Add lavender and salvia behind the wall.

**Fire Pit Garden**
> Place a circular stone fire pit surrounded by pea gravel. Around the perimeter, plant ornamental grasses (Karl Foerster and purple fountain grass), with Adirondack chairs between the grass clusters.

## Lawn & Ground Cover Alternatives

**Clover Lawn**
> Replace the patchy grass with a lush micro-clover lawn, bordered by a natural edge of creeping phlox and wild violets. Add a winding stepping stone path of round slate pavers.

**Moss Garden**
> Transform the shaded area into a moss garden with sheet moss, fern moss, and cushion moss between natural boulders. Add a single stone lantern and a small tsukubai water basin.

**Meadow Wildflowers**
> Convert the open yard into a wildflower meadow with California poppies, lupine, cosmos, and native grasses. Mow a single curved path through the center.

---

## Vertical & Container Gardens

**Living Wall**
> Install a vertical garden panel on the bare fence wall with trailing pothos, ferns, succulents, and flowering bromeliads. Add uplighting at the base.

**Container Patio Garden**
> Arrange large ceramic pots on the patio with dwarf Japanese maple, ornamental grasses, trailing sweet potato vine, and seasonal flowers. Vary pot heights and colors for visual interest.

**Hanging Basket Display**
> Add hanging baskets along the porch overhang with trailing fuchsia, lobelia, ivy geraniums, and Boston ferns. Include a few shepherd's hooks with baskets in the garden bed.

---

## Water Features & Structures

**Pond Garden**
> Add a small naturalistic pond with water lilies, iris, and papyrus along the edges. Place flat fieldstones around the rim and a few koi-colored fish visible in the clear water.

**Dry Creek Bed**
> Create a dry creek bed winding through the yard using smooth river rocks of varying sizes, flanked by blue fescue grass, dwarf nandina, and Japanese forest grass.

**Pergola with Climbing Vines**
> Place a cedar pergola over the patio with wisteria and star jasmine climbing the posts and trailing across the top beams. Add string lights woven through the vines.

## Seasonal Variations

**Spring Bloom**
> Add masses of tulips, daffodils, and hyacinths in the front beds. Place flowering cherry trees with pink blossoms flanking the walkway and a ground cover of creeping phlox in pink and white.

**Fall Color**
> Show the yard in peak autumn: burning bush shrubs in crimson, ornamental grasses in golden plumes, chrysanthemums in orange and burgundy pots on the porch steps, and a sugar maple dropping amber leaves.

**Winter Interest**
> Add plants with winter structure: red-twig dogwood, winterberry holly with bright red berries, ornamental kale in the planters, and a dusting of frost on evergreen topiaries.

## Tips for Best Results

- **Upload everything first** — shoot your fence lines, borders, yard edges from multiple angles before starting. More baseline photos = more complete portfolio.
- **One prompt per project** — keep each project focused on one style. Create separate projects to explore different looks with the same photos.
- **Be specific about plant species** — "Japanese maple (Acer palmatum 'Bloodgood')" gives better results than "red tree"
- **Reference placement** — "along the fence," "in the corner," "flanking the walkway" helps the AI position plants naturally
- **Include scale cues** — "dwarf," "mature," "6-foot tall" helps with realistic proportions
- **Layer heights** — mention tall, medium, and low plants for natural-looking compositions
- **Add non-plant elements** — mulch, rocks, lighting, and furniture make scenes feel complete
- **Iterate to refine** — your first project is a starting point. Create follow-up projects with adjusted prompts based on what you liked and didn't like in the first round.
- **Build your portfolio** — save the best variations from each project to the gallery. Over time you'll have a curated collection showing exactly what you want to plant and where.
